# Author interview

## Intake order

Adapt the order to the material already available. Ask only unresolved questions.

1. What does the author want the reader to experience?
2. What is the reader-facing premise or mystery?
3. What is the story actually examining beneath the plot?
4. Who experiences the conflict, and what do they want now?
5. What force opposes that desire?
6. What concrete event starts the story?
7. What irreversible choice or cost ends it?
8. What setting rules make this story possible?
9. What information must be hidden, mistaken, or revealed?
10. What voice, language, POV, tense, and content boundaries apply?

## Question pattern

For each consequential gap:

1. Briefly state the gap and its downstream effect.
2. Recommend one option with a reason.
3. Offer at most two substantially different alternatives when helpful.
4. Ask one focused question.
5. After the answer, restate the accepted decision only if ambiguity remains.
6. Persist it before moving on.

Avoid turning every answer into another lecture. If the author says yes to a clear proposal, record it and continue.

## Importing files

Classify extracted claims into:

- established facts;
- internal contradictions;
- missing causal links;
- unused but promising material;
- background that never needs to appear on the page;
- narrative requirements: protagonist, opposition, stakes, POV, ending, reveal path.

Report inconsistencies with filenames and exact passages where practical. Ask the author to resolve material conflicts. Do not confuse a mystery intentionally withheld from readers with a gap in author knowledge.

## Stop conditions

End or pause the interview when:

- the next decisions require seeing an outline or sample scene;
- further worldbuilding has no foreseeable narrative consumer;
- the author asks to test a provisional direction;
- one unresolved choice would invalidate substantial downstream work.
