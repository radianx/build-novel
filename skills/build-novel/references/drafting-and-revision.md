# Drafting and revision

## Scene card

Before drafting, know:

- POV, location, time, and entry condition;
- what the POV wants during the scene;
- obstacle or incompatible desire;
- information available at the start;
- turn caused by action or discovery;
- decision, cost, or changed relationship;
- exit condition and continuity dependencies.

Do not force every scene into visible formula. Use the card to diagnose missing movement.

## Voice sheet

Build `STYLE.md` from the author's approved samples and preferences. Record:

- narrative distance, person, tense, and POV rules;
- desired sentence texture and descriptive density;
- dialogue habits for each major character;
- treatment of exposition, humor, violence, intimacy, and technical language;
- recurring patterns to avoid;
- a few short positive and negative examples.

Use broad craft traits instead of imitating a living writer's distinctive voice.
If a character has a regional register, test it especially in private or family conversations, where guarded professional speech is most likely to loosen. Preserve the register through syntax and vocabulary rather than phonetic accent writing.

## Chapter loop

1. Load the relevant scene outline and project facts.
2. Draft the complete chapter in its target file.
3. Re-read without regenerating.
4. Revise for causality, POV leakage, voice, dialogue, rhythm, spatial clarity, and aftermath; apply the narrative-function and model-habit checks below.
5. Confirm the promised scene change occurred.
6. Update continuity records only for facts created and accepted through the manuscript workflow.

## Narrative function of explanation and correction

Use during outlining, drafting, and continuous review of adjacent scenes. Judge an explanation, verification, qualification, or correction by its effect at that point in the reading order:

- **Establish:** keep what the reader needs to understand a mechanism, choice, or consequence for the first time.
- **Transform:** return to familiar information when it changes an option, pressure, relationship, interpretation, or emotional experience. Repeated checks can enact distrust or grief; quiet preparation can earn interest through curiosity, voice, or pleasure before its later payoff.
- **Repeat without change:** compress or omit a restatement, test, or distinction that the reader already understands and that adds no meaningful experience. Do not invent another complication merely to justify keeping the passage.

Check dialogue beyond question-answer mismatches: `assertion → correction → more precise assertion` can become a shared script even when every distinction is valid. Let each speaker's desire, knowledge, and relationship determine whether they clarify, resist, act, joke, remain silent, or leave something unresolved. Vary the dramatic interaction, not just the wording; do not alternate these responses mechanically.

After editing, verify that the reader can still follow the relevant cause, choice, and cost. Preserve technical precision, uncertainty, necessary orientation, interiority, humor, and repetition with a dramatic function. Use no quota of cuts, mandatory acceleration, or preferred kind of ending. This check supports the author's chosen voice; it does not replace it with terseness.

## Natural prose checks

Look selectively for:

- repeated contrastive definitions, including `not X but Y`, `not merely`, and alternatives introduced only to be dismissed;
- paragraphs ending in packaged wisdom or a thematic seal;
- every character producing equally polished aphorisms;
- gestures immediately translated into their exact meaning;
- dialogue that advances without hesitation, evasion, interruption, misunderstanding, or failed attempts;
- technical systems that work only as clean cinematic devices;
- analytical characters counting or measuring irrelevant details to prove intelligence;
- repeated explicit statements of what evidence proves and does not prove;
- generic evaluative adjectives, promises of benefits without costs, and repeated announcements of importance in place of an observable consequence;
- repeated exchanges in which one character answers an adjacent question and the other replies that this was not what they asked;
- several adjacent paragraphs that name technical checks, formats, containers, or transformations after the reader already understands the practical consequence;
- abstract technical nouns where a concrete reader-facing noun such as `folder`, `file`, `record`, or `copy` would preserve the meaning;
- scenes cut the instant their informational function is complete.

Keep good instances. Assess recurring moves with the narrative-function check above; frequency alone does not justify a cut. Do not inject typos or arbitrary awkwardness to simulate humanity.

### Question-answer mismatch

Treat `A asks X → B answers adjacent question Y → A says that was not the question` as a conspicuous rhetorical device, not a neutral dialogue pattern. Keep it only when the evasion itself changes power, relationship, strategy, or stakes. Otherwise let B evade in a character-specific way, answer only the dangerous part, redirect through action or silence, or let A pursue the missing information without naming the conversational mechanism. Search across adjacent chapters: individually defensible instances still become mechanical when repeated.

### Model-habit pass

Use this during the chapter loop and block review. The target is the author's voice and the reader's experience. A detector score or a word blacklist is not an acceptance criterion.

**Literary and fantasy safeguards:** lyricism, solemnity, hyperbole, anaphora, triads, refrains, and ritual language are legitimate choices. Judge their function, effect, and fit with the author's chosen voice before changing them; recurrence alone is not a defect. Atmosphere, musicality, wonder, and emotional resonance can justify a passage without a new fact or plot turn. Preserve deliberate mystery, ambiguity, and unreliable narration; distinguish accidental inconsistency from a character's belief, lie, or faulty memory using the project's canon and POV policy. Keep Spanish dialogue dashes and the target language's literary punctuation. Adapt external guides such as [Humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) as diagnostic prompts, never as automatic bans or instructions to flatten prose. During revision, do not invent lore to repair a stylistic flag; during authorized drafting, invention remains part of the task within the agreed canon and scope.

Read narration, internal speech, dialogue, and embedded documents as separate voices. Look across neighboring passages for repeated rhetorical work even when the wording changes: defining by exclusion, qualifying every claim, announcing a distinction, explaining an emotion immediately after showing it, or ending each paragraph with an interpretation.

- **Contrast and qualification:** retain a negation, caveat, or uncertainty when it changes what a character knows, risks, or chooses. Cut repeated assurances once the scene establishes the limit. Keep uncertainty where the evidence requires it; stylistic revision must preserve the claim's strength and scope.
- **Specificity:** test whether an approving adjective or transition supplies information. When a passage merely rates an action or announces its significance, use the relevant act, object, consequence, or character judgment already supported by the scene. Do not add facts or decorative detail to make a sentence seem specific.
- **Cadence:** listen for repeated sentence lengths, paragraph shapes, and action–explanation–summary sequences. Let deliberation, interruption, urgency, and aftermath shape the syntax. Avoid a new mechanical pattern of alternating long and short sentences or isolated punch lines.
- **Voice and vocabulary:** preserve useful repeated names and plain words. Unnecessary synonyms can obscure an object or make every speaker equally polished. Keep digressions, exclamations, colloquial turns, humor, and awkward exchanges when the chosen voice warrants them. Do not manufacture typos, anecdotes, slang, or punctuation quotas.
- **Revision decision:** identify the passage, the recurring move, and its effect on reading. Make the smallest useful change and reread the surrounding scene. Preserve deliberate repetition, technical labels, documentary register, and character-specific habits when they earn their place. Record project-specific patterns in `STYLE.md`; private manuscript examples stay in that project.

When an author brings a new study, verify the source and model/version comparison before adding a rule. Distinguish measured usage from an editorial interpretation. Translated examples are prompts for inspection, not measured frequencies in the target language. Individual occurrences are not proof of machine authorship, and blanket suppression of punctuation can damage normal dialogue conventions.

**Research anchor, checked 2026-10-05:** Graphite's [AI Tells](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/ai-tells), published September 16, compares 10,000 pre-ChatGPT web articles with 90,000 generated articles. It reports 12,877 overrepresented items, including 3,687 for Astra; Astra's corrective-framing composite is about 12 times the human rate. The [October 1 update](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/ai-tells-opus-5-5-update) reports Astra's “does not establish” at 275 times **Opus 5.5's** rate and “may provide” at 18 times that model's rate. These comparisons use a fixed article-writing prompt; they do not validate a recipe for Spanish fiction. Both reports distinguish model/version effects, and their punctuation findings caution against blanket em-dash removal. The editing procedure above is a craft adaptation, not a validated intervention from those studies.

## Emotional turning-point pass

For deaths, betrayals, irreversible losses, family revelations, and other events meant to carry unusual emotional weight, read the full sequence rather than the announcement alone. Check:

- enough anticipatory space for the reader to understand who or what may be lost;
- physical and informational clarity at the decisive moment;
- reactions specific to each involved character rather than one generic signal of grief;
- silence, practical interruption, denial, or delayed comprehension where immediate eloquence would feel false;
- aftermath before the plot converts the event into information or strategy;
- an observable later change in behavior, relationship, or decision.

Do not enlarge every important event. Add only the missing stage of experience, and preserve shock when compression is intentional.

## Block review

Read several adjacent chapters continuously. Check:

- tension has variation rather than uniform escalation;
- discoveries have human consequences before the next clue;
- relationships change on the page;
- repeated facts and rhetorical moves are reduced;
- time, travel, injuries, objects, and knowledge remain coherent;
- quiet scenes deepen pressure or attachment rather than serving as filler;
- endings vary: action, interruption, decision, unresolved practical task, or earned revelation.

## Reader-accessibility pass

Run this after the complete draft and before general editorial revision. Its purpose is navigation, not simplification.

Review in reading order and mark only passages where the reader must remember or decode more than the scene needs. Check:

- **Reentry:** after a time, place, or POV gap, can the reader identify who is acting, what their present role is, and what situation changed?
- **Function before label:** does a returning person, institution, system, brand, or acronym receive enough functional context before its name carries meaning?
- **Concept load:** are several unfamiliar mechanisms introduced in one paragraph when their causal order could be clearer?
- **Technical threshold:** must the reader understand the term to grasp the choice or consequence? If yes, translate it briefly in context. If no, let it remain texture or remove it.
- **Hierarchy:** when several bodies share authority, can the reader tell who proposes, evaluates, authorizes, operates, and bears the cost?
- **Mystery boundary:** is confusion intentional because the POV lacks information, or accidental because the prose omitted orientation?
- **Spatial footing:** on first entering a consequential setting, or after a chapter break that weakens continuity, can the reader tell what kind of place it is, whether the action is stationary or moving, and which participants are local or remote? Add only the details needed for action, distance, sight, or hearing.
- **Visible schemas:** when characters create a table, board, taxonomy, or procedural list, are all current labels introduced when the structure appears? Delay a label only when discovering it later is an intentional reveal.

Prefer, in order:

1. replacing a label with its function;
2. adding a few functional words;
3. reordering existing information;
4. splitting a dense paragraph;
5. adding a sentence only when the previous options do not hold.

Do not add chapter recaps, a glossary by default, repeated explanations, or definitions the scene already demonstrates. Preserve specialized language that establishes profession, culture, or world. Accessibility is not a lower reading level; it is a lower cost of finding the story inside the complexity.

Do not turn spatial orientation into a room inventory. One functional or sensory anchor is usually enough, and a familiar location does not need to be redescribed every time it returns.

When documenting the pass, list changed chapters, the navigation problem corrected, and important passages deliberately left intact. Do not use a word-count target or a quota of interventions.

## General revision

Review by scene before reviewing by chapter. Ask: did the scene end because its lived consequence was complete, or merely because the next information had been delivered?

Do not expand toward a word-count target. Expand where experience, causality, relationship, or consequence remains compressed. Cut where a passage repeats function.

## Broad structural revision

Use this only when repeated feedback points beyond sentence-level editing.

1. If the diagnosis is uncertain, run a blind baseline panel before reading planning files or changing prose.
2. Map the manuscript in reading order: chapter function, POV, entry pressure, turn, exit pressure, revelations, human consequence, and cognitive load.
3. Read adjacent chapters as blocks. Locate repeated scene engines, consecutive explanatory chapters, revelations without aftermath, passive protagonists, and climaxes whose mechanics obscure their emotional choice.
4. Separate three categories:
   - **structural defect:** order, causality, agency, or distribution prevents the intended experience;
   - **local craft problem:** wording, voice, orientation, or pacing can be repaired inside the scene;
   - **deliberate difficulty:** density, ambiguity, or genre hybridity belongs to the novel's promise.
5. Propose no more large interventions than the diagnosis requires. State the problem, exact structural operation, expected reader effect, and risks before editing.
6. Apply accepted changes one at a time. Update outline, reveal order, attachments, timeline, and knowledge dependencies with each change.
7. Re-read the affected blocks continuously. A successful intervention should improve the named experience without requiring explanatory patches elsewhere.
8. Only after the structure stabilizes, perform continuity and surgical scene or prose editing.
9. Run a blind validation panel with the same neutral brief, profiles, and scoring anchors. After the independent reports are complete, compare whether the old reader effects remain; do not expose previous diagnoses or changed chapters to readers.
10. Close the pass explicitly. Do not invent another reconstruction because the process produced only a few changes.

Useful high-impact operations include showing a character's claimed trait through work instead of testimony, separating emotional testimony from technical demonstration, and alternating adjacent chapters that repeat the same investigative mode. Preserve a chapter when its density has a clear function and readers continue voluntarily.
