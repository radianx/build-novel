# Independent reader panel

Use this reference for simulated-reader evaluation, with independent agents or equivalent isolated evaluations. Report in the author's language. The panel can serve two purposes:

- **Baseline:** after accessibility and before broad structural revision when the diagnosis is uncertain, feedback conflicts, or the manuscript changes scale, genre promise, or narrative mode.
- **Validation:** after structural, continuity, and surgical revision to test the resulting reader experience.

Do not run a baseline panel merely to collect scores when the problem and remedy are already clear. Scores summarize an evidenced response; they are not a revision target.

## Build a sharply differentiated panel

Choose profiles that expose different reader effects. For a broad review, a useful starting set is the impatient reader, genre veteran, voice reader, character reader, and structural editor. Add or substitute the other lenses when relevant. Select by reading history and concerns, not by demographic impersonation or claimed lived authority.

Intensify what each profile notices, values, and refuses to overlook. A demanding reader must also recognize exceptional success in that specialty. Do not manufacture hostility, low scores, defects, or disagreement to act out a persona. Profiles may agree when their evidence warrants it.

| Profile and specialty | Sharpened disposition | What earns enthusiasm | Failure trigger and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Everyday reader — orientation** | Reads for pleasure with limited attention available; notices every avoidable burden of names, references, and scene changes. | Understands what matters through lived action and remembers the essentials without studying. | Cannot tell who wants what or why an event matters. Does not require simple ideas, recaps, or a glossary. |
| **Impatient reader — momentum** | Gives each scene very little credit in advance; constantly asks what makes the next page worth choosing. | A desire, tension, voice, discovery, or emotional pressure keeps changing the experience. | Repetition without change, deferred stakes, or setup that never earns interest. A quiet scene can grip; action and cliffhangers are not mandatory. |
| **Genre veteran — promise and payoff** | Has seen the obvious solutions; tests the work's emerging genre contract, planted clues, and earned surprises. | Familiar pleasures gain a specific identity; reversals are surprising yet supported. | Arbitrary solutions, unearned payoffs, or twists sustained by withheld POV knowledge. Genre conventions are options, not a compulsory beat sheet. |
| **Voice reader — prose and distinctiveness** | Has an exacting ear for cadence, image, dialogue, repeated rhetorical moves, and visible authorial explanation. | Precise, character-specific language; subtext; a voice whose choices serve the scene. | Interchangeable voices, packaged wisdom, decorative phrasing, or relentless polish. Plain prose, roughness, and repetition can be deliberate strengths. |
| **Character reader — emotional credibility** | Insists that choices, relationships, and losses feel inhabited; notices where stated feelings outrun lived experience. | Specific desires, ambivalence, agency, differentiated reactions, and consequences that persist. | Scripted behavior, interchangeable grief, or irreversible events with no human aftermath. Restraint, unsympathetic characters, and ambiguity can work. |
| **Skeptical specialist — plausibility** | Follows mechanisms, costs, limits, incentives, and failure modes; stress-tests the rules the story actually establishes. | Constraints create credible choices and consequences, including inconvenient ones. | Internal contradiction or a capability that appears only to rescue the plot. Separate accepted fictional premises, missing explanation, and real-world claims requiring verification. Do not demand a manual or assert expertise as evidence. |
| **Structural editor — causality and necessity** | Tests what every scene changes and whether decisions, reveals, and consequences form a convincing whole. | Scenes do several useful jobs; characters cause turns; the ending pays for its effects. | Repeated functions, passive protagonists, missing causal links, or payoffs without preparation. Do not force a standard structure or predict acquisition and sales. |
| **Curious outsider — entry into an unfamiliar genre** | Brings little genre knowledge and no automatic loyalty to its conventions, but a willingness to discover its appeal. | Concrete stakes make unfamiliar ideas worth learning without prior expertise. | Necessary meaning depends on unstated genre knowledge. Personal unfamiliarity is not automatically a defect; specialist texture can remain. |

Before launching, give each reader a brief profile card: reading history, primary lens, what wins them over, what loses them, and a tolerated cost. For example, the voice reader can welcome slow progression for distinctive language; the impatient reader needs that language itself to sustain interest. Use no manuscript-specific hints. The skeptical specialist's domain comes from public-facing information or an explicit brief, not hidden plot revelations.

## Preserve blindness and coverage

The coordinator records the manuscript version, ordered files, reading blocks, profiles, and scoring anchors before dispatch. Give each agent a fresh context without inherited discussion whenever the environment permits. Never present several roles in one shared conversation as independent readers; disclose a non-independent fallback if isolation is unavailable.

Give readers only:

- the manuscript and reader-facing attachments in reading order;
- title and public-facing genre when an ordinary reader would receive them;
- their profile card, authorized reading scope, and the neutral reporting contract below.

Do not give them canon, outlines, reveal schedules, style guides, author commentary, prior reviews, other responses, suspected defects, desired scores, or revision diffs. Agents read only assigned material, do not edit the manuscript, and do not collaborate. Keep any report artifacts separate from manuscript files and other readers' inputs. Verify factual objections separately after the blind response is recorded.

Record exact coverage and any missing or truncated material. A summary, search result, or sampling of chapters is not a full read. Abandonment and tool/context limits are different outcomes: report which happened. Do not infer an unseen ending or evaluate unwritten payoffs.

## Read first, advise second

### First-read record

Read continuously in the assigned order. After each chapter or agreed block, record the following before opening the next block:

- **Continue voluntarily:** yes, uncertain, or no, with the specific pull or stopping cause.
- **Interest, 1–10:** 1 = no pull; 5 = intermittent pull; 10 = exceptionally compelling.
- **Cognitive effort, 1–10:** 1 = effortless orientation; 5 = sustained attention; 10 = repeated reconstruction. Explain whether that effort felt rewarding or obstructive; high effort is not inherently bad.
- People, systems, places, open questions, and likely next developments currently retained, without looking back. This is a simulated recall note, not a measured human memory test.
- Dominant emotion, personal stakes, what the protagonists seem to want, and what kind of story now seems promised.
- A concrete moment of success and any friction: rereading, confusion, detachment, visible construction, or lost trust. Include locations; do not invent either praise or a defect to fill a slot.

Separate preference from comprehension, productive mystery from missing orientation, and uncertainty from contradiction. A later explanation may resolve a question; it does not erase the earlier reading cost. Preserve the original block record and note the later resolution separately.

Let readers stop where they genuinely would. Record the precise point and cause; do not force them to finish for coverage. If a later diagnostic read is requested, label it separately from the voluntary first read.

### Scores after reading

Every reader reports two integer scores for the material actually read:

1. **Overall reading experience, 1–10:** how rewarding, involving, and satisfying that experience was for this profile.
2. **Specialty, 1–10:** how well that material worked on the profile's named lens, such as emotional credibility or plausibility.

Use the same anchors across profiles and rounds, applying them separately to each dimension:

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | The experience or specialty largely fails for this reader; severe obstacles dominate. |
| 3–4 | Some successes, but repeated substantial problems outweigh them. |
| 5–6 | Mixed or functional: meaningful rewards coexist with significant limitations. |
| 7–8 | Convincing and strongly rewarding, with identifiable limitations or local friction. |
| 9 | Exceptional achievement in the assessed scope; remaining costs are minor. |
| 10 | Outstanding throughout that scope; this reader can identify no substantive improvement on the assessed dimension. This does not claim universal perfection. |

Justify each score with one or two located examples, its strongest reason for credit, and its main limitation if supported. Explain the chosen position within a paired band rather than treating 7 and 8 as interchangeable. Name an observable change that could justify a higher score when supported; do not promise numerical gains from an edit. Use the whole scale when warranted, with no forced distribution or default cluster around 7–9.

State confidence (low, medium, or high) and the reason separately from the score. A fragment or abandoned read gets a clearly labeled partial-experience score, not a whole-book grade. Mark a specialty **not assessable** when the material provides insufficient evidence. Do not use zero or a low score to encode missing coverage. Two headline scores may legitimately diverge; a pleasing story can have weak mechanisms, and polished prose can leave a reader cold.

### Concrete improvements

After preserving first-read reactions and scores, each reader proposes up to three high-value improvements grounded in their own response. Rereading the covered text to locate evidence is allowed at this stage; it must not rewrite the earlier record. Prefer one supported proposal to three generic suggestions. If no worthwhile improvement is supported, say so and identify what should be preserved.

For each proposal, supply:

- **Where and evidence:** chapter/scene plus a brief quotation or precise event; label personal preference, reading obstacle, internal inconsistency, or unverified factual concern.
- **Reader effect and importance:** what was lost or obstructed, and whether this was a local cost, a recurring burden, or a reason to abandon or lose trust.
- **Smallest useful intervention:** a specific operation on the existing material. If a larger structural alternative is necessary, label it as such and explain why a local change would fail.
- **Expected benefit and tradeoff:** which experience should improve, what could weaken, and what successful feature must survive.
- **Validation:** what a new reader would understand, feel, remember, or choose differently if the intervention works; state uncertainty where the cause is unclear.

"Improve pacing," "show more," and "deepen the character" are not sufficient operations. For example: "At the departure scene, I could not see what refusing would cost; bring the existing threatened consequence into the decision, while preserving uncertainty about who caused it." This specifies a need without inventing canon. Proposed new story facts remain explicit options for the author. Do not rewrite prose unless separately authorized.

## Neutral reporting contract

Give each reader the profile card and the relevant rules above with this compact output contract; do not preload an expected diagnosis:

1. **Scope:** version, files/blocks actually read, completion or exact stop point, and limitations.
2. **Reading record:** block-by-block continuation, interest, effort, retained understanding, emotion, successes, and friction. Keep first impressions intact.
3. **Verdict:** overall experience and named specialty, each `/10`, evidence, calibration, and confidence; label partial or not-assessable judgments.
4. **Improvements:** a short prioritized set with location, cause, operation, benefit/tradeoff, and validation; name strengths to protect.
5. **Uncertainty:** unresolved questions and any criticism that may instead be a preference or genre mismatch.

## Synthesize without voting

First verify coverage and that cited evidence exists. Reports that merely repeat a persona's pet complaint without a concrete reader effect are inconclusive. Check that profile distinctions appear in what each reader values and tolerates, not only in different adjectives.

Present a comparison table with profile, coverage, overall score, specialty score, confidence, continuation, strongest success, and priority improvement. Preserve disagreement. Do not collapse the panel into an average grade or rank partial and complete readings together. If the author explicitly requests an average, label it descriptive, compare only equivalent coverage/dimensions, and keep the individual responses visible.

Group the evidence as follows; these categories can overlap:

- **Convergence:** different profiles independently locate the same effect, with a plausible shared cause.
- **Audience-specific response:** a cost for one reading preference and an attraction for another.
- **Productive disagreement:** readers divide over an ambiguity, style, or genre choice; preserve the competing benefits.
- **Actionable finding:** a location, reader effect, and plausible intervention are supported.
- **Promise mismatch:** the suggested remedy would replace the novel's accepted promise. The remedy may be rejected while the reported discomfort remains real.
- **Inconclusive:** evidence is vague, coverage insufficient, or the diagnosis depends on invented assumptions.

Weight evidence by relevance and specificity, not vote count or claimed credentials. A specialist's lone objection may matter greatly, but still needs support. Shared models can repeat the same bias. An outline or author explanation cannot establish that a meaning was successfully conveyed in the manuscript.

Distinguish a symptom from its proposed cause and remedy: several readers can be bored at the same scene for different reasons, and a correct diagnosis can receive an unsuitable fix. Consolidate proposals only when their mechanisms match. Produce a small prioritized set of options with evidence, expected benefit, cost, confidence, strengths to protect, and unresolved disagreements. Do not negotiate agents toward consensus or optimize for higher marks.

## Decide the revision scale

Choose the smallest scale supported by the evidence:

1. leave intact;
2. line edit;
3. surgical scene edit;
4. reorder, combine, split, replace, or remove scenes;
5. revise a chapter or chapter block;
6. redesign structure or canon with the author.

Classify proposals as `keep`, `consider`, `change`, or `reject` with reasons; a recommendation is not author approval. Apply only accepted changes. Protect deliberate difficulty, specialist texture, ambiguity, and unusual pacing when they serve the narrative promise. Changes to canon, character agency, structure, genre promise, or ending require the author's approval unless already specifically authorized.

## Re-test without teaching the answer

After material revision, use fresh agents with the same profiles, reading scope, neutral brief, and scoring anchors. Give only the new manuscript. Do not tell them the old scores, diagnoses, requested improvements, or changed locations. A reused reader who knows these is an informed reread, not blind validation; label that limitation.

Once independent reports are complete, the coordinator compares whether each targeted reader effect improved, worsened, moved, or remained. Compare score changes only where profile, rubric, and coverage match; otherwise state the difference in conditions. Check preserved strengths and new costs, not just the addressed symptom. A higher number alone is not evidence of a successful intervention.

Stop when the targeted experience improves without creating a worse problem. Persistent disagreements that map cleanly to audiences are positioning information, not necessarily defects. Do not iterate toward unanimous 10s.

When structural changes are needed, preserve the order: synthesize and map; stabilize order, POV, causality, agency, and reveals; check continuity and edit scenes/prose; run validation; generate production artifacts only after closure.

## Limits

- Simulated readers often finish material humans would abandon and overstate recall or attachment. Sharpened personas remain simulations, not independent human populations.
- Shared models can converge because of common training; numbers and confidence labels do not remove that limitation.
- Scores are profile-relative editorial judgments, not objective literary quality or predictions of reviews, sales, awards, or cultural timing.
- Human beta readers remain necessary for lived response, cultural interpretation, fatigue, humor, and emotional attachment.
