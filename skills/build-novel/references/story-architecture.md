# Story architecture

## Author compass

Write concise working answers:

- **About:** the human issue, not the plot machinery.
- **Question:** the difficult question dramatized by the story.
- **Working answer:** what the novel appears to believe; allow ambiguity.
- **Final emotion:** what should remain after the last page.
- **Argument after reading:** what reasonable readers may still disagree about.

## Narrative promise

Express the book in one sentence containing a person or group, a destabilizing event, an active pursuit, and a meaningful consequence. A premise is not necessarily the central reader-facing question; choose what actually pulls the reader forward.

## Character model

For every major character define only useful attributes:

- immediate and long-term desire;
- belief about the world;
- what they misunderstand;
- pressure point and vulnerability;
- secret or withheld information;
- line they think they will not cross;
- decisions that cause plot changes;
- beginning, turn, cost, and end state;
- speech habits and rhetorical limits.

Give recurring supporting characters a distinct narrative function and at least one life context beyond delivering information.

## Opposition

Write the opposing plan step by step, including prerequisites, obstacles, contingencies, blind spots, and costs. For stories without a central antagonist, model competing institutions, deadlines, dependencies, incentives, natural forces, or incompatible legitimate goals.

Allow opposition to produce real benefits. Make costs undeniable and failure modes credible. Invincibility and universal incompetence both destroy tension.

## Worldbuilding

For each system, technology, institution, magic rule, or social norm, record the aspects relevant to the story:

- capability;
- limit;
- cost and access;
- maintenance or enforcement;
- ordinary use;
- abuse;
- known uncertainty;
- who benefits and who absorbs failures.

Internal coherence does not require an exhaustive explanation of magic. Soft magic, mythic causality, numinous forces, and the inexplicable may remain mysterious when they fit the author's narrative promise. Distinguish what the world establishes from what characters believe and what remains unknown; do not invent mechanisms, measurable costs, or technical limits merely to fill this checklist. Check that resolutions honor the story's established possibilities and emotional stakes without requiring the reader to understand every cause.

Prefer contemporary patterns over copied real incidents when fictionalizing current affairs. Include ordinary life so the setting does not exist only in boardrooms and briefings.

## Reveal model

Track each secret with:

| Secret | Truth | Reader learns | Characters who know | Evidence | False interpretation | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Distinguish evidence from inference, but do not make every character verbalize that distinction. Schedule revelations by dramatic effect and causal access, not arbitrary concealment.

## Ending criteria

Know before sustained drafting:

- external outcome;
- protagonist's irreversible decision;
- emotional cost;
- philosophical pressure or answer;
- what remains unresolved intentionally;
- final image or action, provisionally.

Prefer a victory, loss, compromise, or transformation that tests the story's argument. Do not defeat an idea only by killing its spokesperson.
